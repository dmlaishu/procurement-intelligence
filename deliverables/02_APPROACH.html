0. Executive summary

Procurement teams plan each new package with three kinds of knowledge that are kept apart: what happened (contract awards, notices, disbursements), how it was done (procurement plans, bidding documents, supervision reports) and why it worked or failed (completion reports, IEG evaluations). The platform joins the three around a shared spine of identifiers (Project ID, bid reference, supplier, equipment type, country-year).

Why laboratory and diagnostic equipment. It is one of the hardest things health projects buy. Specifications are technical and easy to write around one brand. Analyzers and PCR platforms are often closed systems, so the equipment choice locks in years of reagent and cartridge purchases. Value depends on installation, training, maintenance and a cold chain, not just delivery. COVID-19 then turned it into an emergency market. The Bank's public data already holds thousands of these contracts across more than 80 countries, plus the appraisal, supervision and completion reports that explain what went wrong. But nothing connects a GeneXpert contract in one country to the lesson about cartridge stock-outs in another.

The platform answers one practical question for any new package, through a brief or a chat:

"Given everything the Bank has seen on comparable equipment, who supplies it, what does it cost, how long will the tender take, what could go wrong, and what did past projects learn? Show me the evidence."

Design in five points

  1. Medallion lakehouse with a knowledge graph on top. Raw API records and PDFs land unchanged in Bronze. Typed, de-duplicated tables go to Silver. Gold holds resolved entities (tender, contract, supplier, project, document, lesson) plus an edge table that records how each link was made (exact_key, fuzzy_desc, temporal, llm_extracted) and with what confidence.
  2. Structure-aware document processing. 100+ page reports are split along their own heading hierarchy. Running headers are removed, tables and annexes are typed separately, and the data sheets (ISR rating history, key dates) are parsed deterministically. Retrieval can therefore target Lessons or Key factors sections and cite the exact page.
  3. Censoring-aware timeline benchmarks plus gradient-boosted risk models. Tenders still open are kept as censored observations (Kaplan-Meier) rather than dropped. Quantile models give a P50 and P80 per package. Classifiers score single-bid and re-tender risk. All models are validated out-of-time.
  4. Evidence-first agentic retrieval. Every tool result is registered as citable evidence before any text is written. Statements are composed from that evidence, and a verifier drops any statement whose numbers, quotes or citations are not backed by what it cites. An LLM may rephrase, but cannot add facts.
  5. Closed loop. New notices, awards and evaluations flow in incrementally through Change Data Feed. The vector index re-embeds only what changed, and realised award dates score the models' past predictions. Retraining is triggered by data volume, drift or coverage decay, behind a quality gate.
  6. A domain layer and a chatbot on top. An equipment taxonomy (14 classes, patterns in four languages) turns free-text contract descriptions into a navigable hierarchy: Region → Country → Project → Tender → Contract → Supplier → Equipment type. A chatbot routes each question to the tool that can answer it exactly: SQL for prices, suppliers and spend; survival benchmarks for timelines; retrieval for lessons; the graph for relationships. The same verifier then checks every sentence.

What the prototype shows on real data (§7 has the details)

  • Data spine: 14,613 contract rows, 15,158 notices, 179 projects and 131 project reports (ICR, ISR, PAD, PPAR) were joined into a 115,327-edge knowledge graph. 67% of contracts link to their tender, 100% to their project.
  • Timeline forecasts: out-of-time, the package-level model's median error is 47 days, against 56 for cohort benchmarks (17% better) and 73 for a single median. P80 coverage is 0.76.
  • Domain coverage: 2,220 laboratory and diagnostic equipment contracts worth US$1,375M in 91 countries and 179 projects, from 1,277 resolved suppliers.
  • Censoring matters: for Goods / RFB tenders, ignoring still-open tenders understates the median cycle by 8% (140 vs 153 days).
  • Chatbot: on 16 labelled questions it chose the right tool for 100%, ranked and aggregate answers matched an independent query in 6 of 6 checks, and 100% of its statements are cited.
  • Retrieval: risk-topic precision@5 0.75 (random 0.27). A project's own procurement history retrieves its own evaluation reports in the top 10 for 88% of projects.
  • Faithfulness: 360 statements across 25 generated briefs, 100% with valid citations. The verifier caught 124 of 125 injected hallucinations (fake numbers, quotes, pages, citations); 98% of extracted lessons have quotes verified against source.
  • Document Q&A by country: users pick a country and a category and ask their own question; answers come from that country's project documents with sources (Kenya, India, Nigeria: 111 documents, 4,761 passages). 10/10 evaluation questions passed; a new country is one command and does not rebuild the others (§3.9).
  • On Databricks: every notebook ran end to end on 2026-10-27 with results matching the prototype (2,221 lab contract rows, 733 LLM-extracted lessons 97% quote-verified, models registered as @champion, document Q&A 10/10 on Vector Search). Why a lakehouse plus an edge table, and not a graph database alone? Most questions are analytical (benchmarks, cohorts, model features) and run best as SQL over columnar Delta tables. The graph questions ("which lessons apply to contracts like this one?") are 2-4 hop traversals that SQL joins over kg_edges handle at this scale. The edge table can be exported to a graph engine (GraphFrames, Neo4j) unchanged if multi-hop analytics grow. Everything sits under one governance model: Unity Catalog grants, lineage and audit cover tables, PDFs, functions, the vector index and the models.

Domain-first selection. The domain is defined by what was bought, not by sector codes. Ingestion pulls every Goods contract in Health-practice projects, classifies each item (§2.7), keeps the projects that bought laboratory or diagnostic equipment, then pulls those projects' notices and documents. The same code can be pointed at another domain (the pipeline also has an India roads scope and a three-country scope) by changing one configuration block.

Prototype volumes (FY2015 onwards): 14,613 contract-supplier rows; 10,403 tenders built from notices; 179 projects; 131 project documents (ICR 17, ISR 36, PAD 30, PPAR 0).

2.2 ETL pattern

  • Bronze is immutable and replayable. Each record keeps the exact JSON as returned (raw_json), plus the ingestion key and timestamp. Parsing rules can change later without calling the APIs again, and any Gold value can be traced back to its source record.
  • Idempotent and incremental. Every write is a MERGE on the natural key, updating only when raw_json changed. Change Data Feed is on, so downstream steps (chunking, embedding, lesson extraction) process only new or changed rows. PDFs are downloaded once into a governed Volume.
  • Resilient API access. Rate-limited sources get backoff that honours the server's retry-after (FinancesOne allows only a few calls per minute). Pages are fetched newest-first with a stopping date. Partial failures are logged to ops.app_logs and picked up by the next run.
  • Data contracts. Expectations on Silver (Lakeflow Declarative Pipelines in production) cover:
  • dates parse;
  • amounts ≥ 0;
  • the procurement category is in the controlled list;
  • project IDs match ^P\d{6}$;
  • reference keys are ≥ 5 characters after normalisation.

Violations are quarantined, not dropped silently. - Semi-structured HTML is parsed into structure. Contract Award notices carry a rich HTML body. It is parsed into notification date, contract duration, signed price, and awarded / evaluated / rejected bidders with WB supplier IDs and rejection reasons. This is the only public source of bidder counts and rejection reasons, which are the competition signals the models use.

Every edge row is (src_type, src_id, rel, dst_type, dst_id, method, confidence, valid_from, evidence). valid_from supports temporal questions such as "what did we know when this tender opened?", which the models need to avoid leakage. Worked example: a supplier's contract links to a specific ICR lesson in three hops.

-- Lessons from the reports of projects where this supplier delivered PCR / GeneXpert equipment
SELECT l.lesson, l.evidence_quote, l.title, l.page_start
FROM gold.kg_edges a                                   -- CONTRACT -AWARDED_TO-> SUPPLIER
JOIN gold.kg_edges f ON f.src_id = a.src_id AND f.rel = 'FINANCED_BY'          -- CONTRACT -> PROJECT
JOIN gold.lessons  l ON l.project_id = f.dst_id AND l.quote_verified
JOIN gold.fact_contract c ON c.wb_contract_number = a.src_id
                         AND c.item_type = 'Molecular diagnostics (PCR, GeneXpert)'
WHERE a.rel = 'AWARDED_TO' AND a.dst_id = :supplier_key;

In the prototype the graph holds 115,327 edges.

Scalability. The full public corpus is about 300k contract rows, about 425k notices, about 22k projects and tens of thousands of project documents. That is small for Delta. Tables are clustered by country_iso3 and year (liquid clustering), and the edge table by (rel, dst_id) for reverse lookups. Supplier resolution uses blocking, so it is near-linear. Chunk embedding is the only heavy step, and CDF keeps it incremental.

2.5 Long-document parsing, chunking and embedding

ICRs and PADs run to 60-150 pages. The procurement signal is concentrated in a few sections (Key factors that affected implementation, Lessons, Fiduciary compliance, procurement annexes), and every page repeats a header. The pipeline:

  1. Text extraction. Text-layer PDFs (most WB documents since about 2010) use pypdf, keeping line breaks. Scanned or table-heavy pages use layout-aware parsing (ai_parse_document on Databricks) and OCR. They are detected by a words-per-page threshold.
  2. Running header and footer removal. Short lines that repeat on ≥ 30% of pages, with digits normalised ("Page 16") are removed.
  3. Heading hierarchy. Report numbering is detected: Roman for level 1 (V. LESSONS AND RECOMMENDATIONS), letters for level 2 (B. KEY FACTORS DURING IMPLEMENTATION), and ANNEX n. Each chunk carries its section path.
  4. Typed, bounded chunks.
  5. Chunks never cross a section boundary and hold ≤ 450 words with 60 words of overlap.
  6. Each is typed narrative, table (number-dense lines) or annex.
  7. Each is classified into a section_kind: lessons, key_factors, procurement_fiduciary, risk, ratings, results, design, annex, other.
  8. Structured extraction for data sheets. The ICR data sheet holds the complete ISR rating history, key dates (planned vs actual closing, restructurings) and final ratings. ISRs hold current and previous DO/IP ratings. These are parsed with deterministic rules, not an LLM, because they are tables with known layouts and exact values matter.
  9. Contextual embedding text. Each chunk is embedded as doc type | project | country | sector | date | section path + text. A passage that never names the country or sector still matches queries about them.
  10. Embedding and index. databricks-gte-large-en (1024-d) runs in a Delta Sync index on the chunk table. Filter columns are synced (section_kind, content_type, doc_type_code, country_iso3, project_id, major_sector) and queries run hybrid (semantic + keyword), so P-codes and terms like "re-tender" match exactly.
  11. Small-to-big context. Retrieval returns chunks. The copilot can expand a hit to its whole section for the LLM, which also gives the reader the section path.

Prototype: 131 reports → 6,187 chunks. ISR rating histories were parsed from ICR data sheets (34 projects with dated rating evidence), and 150 lessons were extracted from Lessons / Key factors sections, 98% with verified quotes.

2.6 Missing and incomplete reporting

Gap Handling
CPIA not published for non-IDA countries (e.g. India) kept NULL plus a cpia_missing flag; WGI covers all countries; gradient boosting handles NULL natively
Governance scores published with a lag forward-filled ≤ 3 years, never back-filled (that would leak the future)
Tenders with no award yet right-censored, not dropped (Kaplan-Meier); dropping them would make benchmarks too optimistic. A tender with no award notice after 2 years is not "still open" (most likely cancelled, or the award was never published), so it is excluded as unknown outcome and counted, rather than censored at a multi-year age. Prototype: 1,068 tenders with invitation and award, 122 still open (censored), 442 with no award after 2 years (excluded as unknown outcome). For Goods / RFB the censoring-aware median is 153 days against 140 counting completed tenders only
Awards without an invitation notice (RFQ, direct selection, individual consultants) contribute to supplier and competition statistics, excluded from cycle-time targets
Award notices published late or amended earliest notification date used; amended notices flagged (award_amended_flag)
Documents only partly machine-readable words-per-page check routes them to OCR / layout parsing; a chunk without a verified quote is never served as a lesson
Thin cells (e.g. microscopes in one country) benchmark fallback hierarchy: country + category + method → equipment type + method → equipment type → category + method → category, with n always shown; abstain under 15
Descriptions in several languages, garbled accents, or with no content ("Lot 3") multilingual, accent-tolerant patterns; contracts that cannot be classified stay "Other goods" and are counted, never guessed

3. Module 2: Predictive and prescriptive engines

3.1 Targets, aligned to procurement stages

Planning Tender cycle time invitation (IFB/REOI) date → notification of award; open tenders censored
Tender design Competition risk single responsive bid (competitive methods only), from award notice bidder lists

3.2 Features (planning-time only, leakage-controlled)

Group Features
Package category, equipment type, method, review type (prior/post), estimated amount (log), bid window days, invitation month, description keywords (supply, consulting, IT, vehicles, lots), description length
Project commitment (log), project age at invitation, environmental category, E&S risk rating, major sector, region
Institutional CPIA public-sector management, transparency, budget and financial management; WGI government effectiveness, regulatory quality, control of corruption; missing flags

History features are computed with a strict as-of cut (only tenders awarded before the invitation date), so the backtest is honest.

3.3 Models

  • Benchmarks: Kaplan-Meier survival curves per cohort, reporting the median and P80 with n and the number censored. This is the transparent, explainable baseline planners see first.
  • Package forecast: gradient-boosted quantile regression (P50 and P80 of log-days). Quantiles answer the planner's question directly ("what date can I commit to?") and are robust to the heavy right tail.
  • Risk scores: gradient-boosted classifiers for single-bid, re-tender and award-amendment risk. Training uses class-balanced weights, then probabilities are prior-corrected so they read as calibrated probabilities against the historical base rate.
  • Extension: for in-flight monitoring, a discrete-time hazard model (or Cox with time-varying covariates) updates the probability of award in the next 30 days as the tender ages (§3.6). For packaging decisions, a Bayesian hierarchical model pools thin country × category cells toward the regional mean.

3.4 Validation (out-of-time)

Training uses tenders opened before 2024-01-01; testing uses tenders opened afterwards. The test set is restricted to tenders that had at least a year to complete, so it is not biased toward fast tenders.

Reading the results honestly: the timeline model clearly adds value over benchmarks. The risk classifiers are only weakly to moderately predictive on public data and are shown as indicators, not decisions. Their base-rate shifts between periods are exactly the drift the retraining triggers are for. Re-tender and amendment need internal records (STEP) to become reliable.

3.5 Explainability

Global drivers come from permutation importance on the test period. Per-package drivers use SHAP in production. Each brief shows the benchmark beside the model estimate, so a planner can see when and why the model departs from history.

3.7 Agentic retrieval (RAG) for a new procurement package

 package (country, category, method, estimate, description, planned dates)
   │
   ├─ PLAN      sub-queries: package description; one per elevated risk (single bid / re-tender / delay);
   │            country-specific variant; stage-specific variant (planning, tender design …)
   ├─ ACT       tools (Unity Catalog functions + models):
   │              proc_benchmark          → B  Kaplan-Meier cohort with fallback and n
   │              @champion models        → M  P50 / P80, risk probabilities vs base rates
   │              proc_similar_tenders    → T  what comparable packages did (method, days, bidders, re-tender)
   │              proc_search_lessons     → L  quote-verified lessons (hybrid vector search, section_kind filter)
   │              proc_search_passages    → P  supporting passages from PAD/ISR/ICR/PPAR
   │              proc_project_evidence   → R  conflict-resolved rating trajectory of the lesson's project
   │              proc_country_context    → G  CPIA / WGI context
   ├─ FUSE      reciprocal-rank fusion across sub-queries; boosts for same country / sector / procurement relevance;
   │            diversity cap (≤ 2 items per report); (production) cross-encoder rerank
   ├─ REGISTER  every row becomes evidence with an id, its source, page and URL
   ├─ COMPOSE   statements built only from registered evidence; derived numbers (e.g. date = invitation + P80)
   │            become their own evidence item (D) showing the calculation
   ├─ VERIFY    §4 gate → statements that fail are dropped and logged
   └─ OUTPUT    brief: Schedule · Market approach · Risks · Lessons · Supporting evidence · Evidence list

Prototype brief for a new package, Supply, installation and commissioning of automated real-time PCR systems and GeneXpert analyzers for regional public health laboratories, Lot 2 (Goods, RFB, KEN, est. US$2,500,000) (abridged):

Schedule - Historical benchmark (Molecular diagnostics (PCR, GeneXpert) tenders using RFB (all countries)): half of tenders reach award within 124 days of the invitation and 80% within 240 days. B1 - Counting only completed tenders would understate the median by 6 days, because slow tenders are still open. 4.2 Resolving conflicting institutional evidence

Supervision ratings (ISR, every six months) and completion evaluations (ICR, IEG) often disagree. That is expected: they are measured at different times with different information. The rules:

  1. Same dimension only. Compare like with like: DO rating vs outcome rating; IP rating vs implementation performance. Different dimensions are shown side by side, never "resolved".
  2. Authority hierarchy for the final judgement. Independent ex-post (IEG PPAR) > independent validation (IEG ICRR)

    self-evaluation at completion (ICR) > in-flight supervision (ISR) > appraisal expectation (PAD). Completed and independent evaluations see the whole outcome; mid-term ratings are snapshots.

  3. Recency within the same authority.
  4. Never discard the loser. The resolved record keeps the full dated trajectory and a conflict flag (evidence on both sides of the satisfactory line). A mid-term crisis followed by recovery is itself a lesson about what turned it around.
  5. Field-level source of record for transactional conflicts. Signed amount and supplier come from the contract awards dataset. Bidder list and notification date come from the award notice. Dates disagreeing by more than 30 days are flagged for review rather than averaged.
  6. Show it to the user. When a lesson's project had conflicting ratings, the brief says so (e.g. "supervision ratings fell to Moderately Unsatisfactory before the final ICR rating of Satisfactory"), with both sources cited.

Prototype: 34 projects have dated rating evidence from several sources. In 8 the supervision ratings and the final rating fall on opposite sides of the satisfactory line. All are resolved with their trajectory kept, and briefs cite both sides when such a project's lesson is used.
